ranwhat / Privacy

Privacy

Privacy: the tool and this website

The first part is about the ranwhat command-line tool, the second about the ranwhat.com website, and the third about the optional account at account.ranwhat.com.

01 / Boundary

Nothing leaves the machine unless you ask.

Not a policy but an architecture. Anything touching a secret runs locally. live, update, login, whoami, logout and the --pull-usage flag go online, and only when you run them.

Stays local

Credentials, prompts, tool arguments, file contents, customer records. The CLI holds a provider token in memory for one call and never writes it down. The only token it stores is your own ranwhat token, at ~/.ranwhat/token (mode 0600): the one ranwhat login saves for this machine, or a subscription token you save with update --save-token. Beside it, ~/.ranwhat/account.json (mode 0600) notes the plan, organisation and email address the server last gave for that token, filed under the token’s SHA-256 and never the token, so the CLI knows whether to mention Plus. logout deletes both.

Goes online, when you ask

live and --pull-usage call the provider that issued each token, and nothing else. update sends your token to feed.ranwhat.com and nothing else. A token made by ranwhat login belongs to one machine, and a CI token to one pipeline, so we know the day each last used its token, and nothing finer. login, whoami and logout talk to the same host: login to get a code and then this machine’s token, the other two with that token. None of them sends a hostname, operating system, user name or machine identifier. A hosted record of what your agents did, when one exists, would receive derived metadata only: what tool, what authority, whether it was reversible, with payloads hashed in place. None exists yet, and the account described below holds no agent data at all.

Read-only Scans introspect scopes. They never exercise a permission, and never request a write-scoped credential to run.
Auditable Pure Python standard library. No dependency tree to review before you point it at your own keys.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask what we hold about you, correct it, have it deleted, or object to it being processed at all. Write to the address above. If we handle that badly you can complain to AZOP, the Croatian data protection authority.

Changes

When this notice changes the commit history shows exactly what changed and when, which is more than most privacy pages can offer. It lives in the same public repository as the site.

The install is not tracked

Installing ranwhat, and running check, watch, clean, scan or demo, sends us nothing. There is no licence check, no version ping, no crash reporter. update, login, whoami and logout are the only commands that talk to our server, and only when you run them. If you link a machine with ranwhat login, we know that machine exists and the day its token was last used. Beyond that we do not know how many people use it, which is occasionally inconvenient and entirely the point.

03 / Account

And what the account keeps.

The account at account.ranwhat.com is optional. It manages Plus for an organisation: its members, the machines linked with ranwhat login, CI tokens and billing. Nothing ranwhat does on your machine needs it.

Its pages account.ranwhat.com is a separate site from ranwhat.com. Its pages load none of ranwhat.com’s scripts: not Google Analytics, not X’s pixel and not Cloudflare Web Analytics. The only scripts that run there are Cloudflare Turnstile, on the pages that email a code, and our own passkey script, on the two pages that add a passkey or sign in with one. The account stores no IP address and no user agent. Cloudflare logs requests to it as it does for this site (Server logs, above).
Cookies Four, set only on account.ranwhat.com, never sent to ranwhat.com, and each only when you use what it is for: __Host-rw_session, your signed-in session, set when you sign in and kept at most 30 days (the session also ends after 14 days unused, or when you sign out); __Host-rw_signin, which ties a sign-in form and its emailed code to your browser, set when such a form is first shown and kept an hour, or until you are signed in; __Host-rw_oauth, set when you choose Google or GitHub, for 10 minutes; and __Host-rw_invite, set when you open an invite link, for an hour, so you come back to it after signing in. Each holds random bytes, of which we keep only a hash. They are strictly necessary, so they need no consent (art. 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive, and art. 100 of the Croatian Electronic Communications Act).
Your account Your email address, once a code typed from that inbox, or Google for an address it gives out, has shown it is yours; when the account was made and when you last signed in; and, if you set one, your password as a PBKDF2 hash, never the password itself. A password you choose is first checked against passwords known from data breaches, with Have I Been Pwned: our Worker sends it only the first five characters of the password’s SHA-1 hash, never the password. All of this is kept until the account is deleted.
Google and GitHub Optional ways to sign in. If you use one, your browser goes to Google or GitHub, which asks you to let ranwhat read your email address (and, on GitHub, your profile). Our Worker then exchanges a one-time code with them, reads your account’s ID there and the address they say is verified (on GitHub, your primary verified address), and keeps only those two, with when the link was made and last used. It stores no Google or GitHub token: GitHub’s is used for those two reads and then dropped. While you are away at the provider, we keep only hashes of the random values that tie your return to your browser, for 10 minutes. If you unlink one, we keep its ID, without an address, so that it cannot link itself to your account again, for as long as the account exists.
Passkeys If you add one: its credential ID, its public key, its signature counter, whether it is backed up, the name you give it, when you added it, and the day it last signed you in. Nothing about the device, and no attestation is asked for. The account also gets a random passkey handle, which names nothing else.
Organisations Every account starts with a personal organisation it owns. An organisation keeps its name, its members and their roles, and, from its first checkout, the ID of its Stripe customer record. Everyone in it sees the other members’ email addresses, and the machines linked to it with who linked each. An invite keeps the invited address while it waits, seven days at most, and the address is cleared once the invite is used, taken back or out of date. The invite email tells the person invited who sent it and which organisation it is for.
Machines For each machine linked with ranwhat login, and each CI token: the name given to it on the account page, who linked or made it and when, and the day it was last used, to the day and no finer. The token itself is kept only as its SHA-256 hash. While a terminal waits to be approved, we keep the country Cloudflare saw its request come from, to show on the approval page, and delete it with the request about an hour after the request’s 10 minutes run out. A terminal’s token unused for 90 days is revoked. A revoked machine leaves the list, but its record, name included, is kept for as long as the organisation is, so its history still makes sense.
Security history What happened and when: sign-ins and sign-outs, changes to how you sign in, machines and CI tokens linked, made, named or revoked, membership and role changes, and upgrades and billing opened. You see your own on the account page, and an organisation’s owners and admins see its membership changes. It is kept for 13 months, and never holds a code, a token, an email address, an IP address or a user agent. A session keeps a hash of its cookie and when it was made, last used and last confirmed with a code, and is deleted once it ends.
Codes and limits When you ask for an emailed code, we keep the address you typed, a keyed hash of the code and, if you are making an account with a password, the hash of that password, for the code’s 10 minutes. The record is deleted within a quarter of an hour of the code being used or running out, so an address nobody confirms does not stay. The email has no link: the code is typed. To stop abuse, requests are counted per network, per address, per account and per organisation under a keyed hash, never the IP address or the address itself, and each count is deleted a day after it starts.
Email Resend sends the account’s email: sign-in, confirmation and password reset codes, a notice when a Google or GitHub account or a passkey is added to yours, invites, and the email that says Plus is on. We keep no copy of what it sends. Resend may process it in the United States, as described in Resend’s privacy policy.
Where In a Cloudflare D1 database on our Cloudflare account, the same one the release list and the feed use.
Legal basis The contract you enter by making an account (GDPR art. 6(1)(b)), for the account and what it needs to work: your address, how you sign in, sessions, organisations and machines. Our legitimate interest in keeping accounts secure (art. 6(1)(f)), for the security history, the limits, the country on a terminal’s request and the breached-password check; and the organisation’s legitimate interest in inviting someone, for an invited address while the invite waits. The IDs of a sale are kept as Payments, above, says.
Deleting On the account page you can leave an organisation, revoke a machine, unlink Google or GitHub, remove a passkey or a password, and sign out everywhere. There is no button that deletes the account itself yet: write to hello@ranwhat.com from its address and we delete it by hand, with what belongs to it alone, keeping only the IDs of a sale as its record.
