Privacy

Privacy: the tool and this website

The first half is about the ranwhat command-line tool. The second is about ranwhat.com.

01 / Boundary

Nothing leaves the machine unless you ask.

Not a policy but an architecture. Anything touching a secret runs locally. live, update and the --pull-usage flag go online, and only when you run them.

Stays local

Credentials, prompts, tool arguments, file contents, customer records. The CLI holds a provider token in memory for one call and never writes it down. The only token it stores is your own subscription token, and only if you run update --save-token (mode 0600).

Goes online, when you ask

live and --pull-usage call the provider that issued each token, and nothing else. update, for Plus subscribers, sends the subscription token to feed.ranwhat.com and nothing else. A hosted dashboard, when one exists, would receive derived metadata only: what tool, what authority, whether it was reversible, with payloads hashed in place.

Read-only Scans introspect scopes. They never exercise a permission, and never request a write-scoped credential to run.
Auditable Pure Python standard library. No dependency tree to review before you point it at your own keys.

Ad measurement We advertise on X, and ask before measuring whether it works. If you allow it, X’s pixel loads from static.ads-twitter.com and tells X which pages you visited, with your IP address, your browser details and X’s own cookies, so X can connect the visit to an ad it showed you and, if you are signed in to X, to your account. If you then send the contact form, our server tells X that a message was sent, with the ad-click ID if an ad brought you here, your IP address and your browser. If you decline, or your browser sends Global Privacy Control, nothing from X loads. Change your answer any time with Privacy choices, also in the footer. What X does with it is in X’s privacy policy.
Forms The contact form sends your email address, your topic and your message to a Cloudflare Worker on this domain, which checks the challenge and forwards the message to our mailbox. The Worker keeps no copy. What arrives is kept as described under Email below. The signup for release emails is described in the next row.
Release emails If you sign up for release emails, on the updates page or the home page, your address goes to the same Worker, which checks the challenge and keeps the address, with the time you signed up, in a database on our Cloudflare account until you confirm. Resend then sends you one email with a link to confirm: you are on the list only once you press the button that link leads to, and an address nobody confirms is deleted after a week. Once you confirm, the address moves to Resend, which keeps the list and sends the release emails, and we delete our copy. Every release email has a link to unsubscribe, which takes you off the list at once. We do not switch on Resend’s open or click tracking. We use the list to announce releases and nothing else, and never sell or share it. Resend may store it in the United States. The legal basis is your consent (GDPR art. 6(1)(a)); withdraw it with the unsubscribe link or by writing to us. Resend’s handling is described in Resend’s privacy policy. The RSS feed carries the same news and asks nothing of you.
Server logs Cloudflare hosts and serves the site. It logs request metadata, including your IP address, to deliver pages and absorb abuse. That is our legitimate interest in running a website, and the logs are not used to profile anyone.
Page views Cloudflare Web Analytics counts visits for everyone, whatever you answer above. Your browser loads Cloudflare’s script from static.cloudflareinsights.com, which sends cloudflareinsights.com the page you view, the page that brought you, your browser and device, and how long the page took to load; Cloudflare adds your country from your IP address. It sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser, and Cloudflare says it does not fingerprint visitors. That is our legitimate interest in knowing which pages are read, and it is not used to profile anyone.
Third parties Cloudflare Turnstile, on the contact page, and on the home and updates pages once you start typing an address into the signup form: the challenge that keeps the forms from being used by bots. Google Analytics and X, each only if you allow it (above). Maidensail, on the home page only: the "Featured on Maidensail" badge is an image from maidensail.com, so your browser asks maidensail.com for it, which sees your IP address and browser like any website you load something from. We keep the message and your address for as long as the conversation is useful, and delete it when it is not. Writing to us does not add you to any list.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask what we hold about you, correct it, have it deleted, or object to it being processed at all. Write to the address above. If we handle that badly you can complain to AZOP, the Croatian data protection authority.

Changes

When this notice changes the commit history shows exactly what changed and when, which is more than most privacy pages can offer. It lives in the same public repository as the site.

The install is not tracked

Installing ranwhat, and running check, watch, clean, scan or demo, sends us nothing. There is no licence check, no version ping, no crash reporter. update is the one command that talks to our server, and only when you run it. We do not know how many people use it, which is occasionally inconvenient and entirely the point.
