01 / Boundary

Nothing leaves the machine.

Not a policy but an architecture. Anything touching a secret runs locally; live introspection talks only to the credential's own issuer.

Stays local

Credentials, prompts, tool arguments, file contents, customer records. The CLI holds a token in memory for one call and never writes it down.

Would ever leave

Nothing today. When a hosted dashboard exists it receives derived metadata only: what tool, what authority, whether it was reversible, with payloads hashed in place.

Read-only Scans introspect scopes. They never exercise a permission, and never request a write-scoped credential to run.
Auditable Pure Python standard library. No dependency tree to review before you point it at your own keys.

Cookies None of ours. Google Analytics and X’s ad pixel set their own, each only if you allow that one (next two rows). Your answer is kept in your browser’s local storage so the question is asked once.
Visit statistics If you allow it, Google Analytics loads Google’s gtag.js and records the pages you view, the page or campaign that brought you, your device, browser and screen, and an approximate location that Google works out from your IP address. Google says Analytics does not log or store the IP address itself. It sets two cookies on this domain, _ga and _ga_MTR111ZTTE, which last up to two years so a repeat visit can be counted. Google signals and ad personalisation are switched off, so this is used to count visits, not to advertise to you. The data is kept for two months. Google may process it in the United States under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. The legal basis is your consent (GDPR art. 6(1)(a), and art. 100 of the Croatian Electronic Communications Act). Withdraw it any time with Privacy choices. Google’s handling is described in Google’s privacy policy.
Ad measurement We advertise on X, and ask before measuring whether it works. If you allow it, X’s pixel loads from static.ads-twitter.com and tells X which pages you visited, with your IP address, your browser details and X’s own cookies, so X can connect the visit to an ad it showed you and, if you are signed in to X, to your account. If you then send the contact form, our server tells X that a message was sent, with the ad-click ID if an ad brought you here, your IP address and your browser. Never the message and never your email address. The legal basis is your consent (GDPR art. 6(1)(a), and art. 100 of the Croatian Electronic Communications Act). If you decline, or your browser sends Global Privacy Control, nothing from X loads. Change your answer any time with Privacy choices, also in the footer. What X does with it is in X’s privacy policy.
Forms The contact form sends your email address, your topic and your message to a Cloudflare Worker on this domain, which checks the challenge and forwards the message to our mailbox. The Worker keeps no copy. What arrives is kept as described under Email below.
Server logs Cloudflare hosts and serves the site. It logs request metadata, including your IP address, to deliver pages and absorb abuse. That is our legitimate interest in running a website, and the logs are not used to profile anyone.
Third parties Cloudflare Turnstile, on the contact page only: the challenge that keeps the form from being used by bots. It loads from challenges.cloudflare.com and reads signals from your browser to tell a person from a script. Google Analytics and X, each only if you allow it (above). Nothing else loads from anywhere else. Typefaces are served from this domain rather than Google Fonts.
Email Mail, whether written directly or sent through the form, is kept in a Gmail mailbox, so Google holds it too and may store it outside the EU. We keep the message and your address for as long as the conversation is useful, and delete it when it is not. We do not add you to any list.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask what we hold about you, correct it, have it deleted, or object to it being processed at all. Write to the address above. If we handle that badly you can complain to AZOP, the Croatian data protection authority.

Changes

When this notice changes the commit history shows exactly what changed and when, which is more than most privacy pages can offer. It lives in the same public repository as the site.

The install is not tracked

Installing or running ranwhat sends us nothing. There is no licence check, no version ping, no crash reporter. We do not know how many people use it, which is occasionally inconvenient and entirely the point.
