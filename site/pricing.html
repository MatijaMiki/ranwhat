The rule

We don't charge per finding.

A tool paid by the alert has a financial reason to cry wolf, and the only thing this product really sells is being believed.

The first watch build flagged six findings on real history, and three were false positives. A later release reported fifteen findings on one machine's 90-day history. Eleven were deletions of build and temp directories: all true, none worth reading. After the fix, the same history gave four. Under per-finding pricing both fixes would have cost us money, which is exactly why the meter isn't there.

Nothing is held back from the free tier to make the paid ones look better. Every rule, every source and every report runs locally, and the code is there to check. You are not paying for features withheld from a binary; you are paying because retention, correlation and a record you cannot edit yourself all need someone else to run them.

What you pay One flat price per organisation. Not per agent, not per seat, not per machine.
Not metered Findings, alerts, seats, agents, scans, rules, reports, retention queries.
Why flat A catalogue is worth the same to you whether two machines read it or two hundred. Charging by agent would only teach you to under-report how many you run, which is the opposite of what this tool is for.
Questions

Before you pay.

Do you see our prompts or data?

No, and not as a policy. The collector will run inside your network and hash payloads in place. What reaches us will be which tool ran, what authority it held, and whether it was reversible.

What happens if we stop paying?

Local keeps working, because it's the same open-source code. You export your records; we don't hold them hostage to a renewal.

We have two hundred agents.

Then you pay the same €12 as someone with two. The price is per organisation, so scale is not a reason to talk to us. Needing records a third party will accept is.

Can we self-host the evidence store?

No, and that is the point rather than a limitation. A hash chain you control is one you can rewrite, so an underwriter should ignore it. The records are worth something precisely because we hold them and you cannot quietly change them. The collector that will run in your network is yours; the chain is deliberately not.

How do we cancel?

With Manage billing on your account at account.ranwhat.com, or from the billing page, any time. The feed keeps working to the end of the period you paid for. Changed your mind in the first 14 days? Write to us and the first payment is refunded in full. The terms have the rest.

Where does the token go?

Run uvx ranwhat login once on each machine: it prints a code to type at ranwhat.com/device, signed in to your account, and that machine gets a token of its own, which ranwhat update then uses. Pipelines use a CI token from your account page. Anyone who bought Plus before accounts keeps the token they were emailed, saved once with ranwhat update --save-token.

Honest status

Free ships today and is what the GitHub repository contains. Of Plus, the catalogue feed is live: subscribe, and ranwhat update keeps your catalogue current from feed.ranwhat.com as providers add scopes. So are CI tokens and members, on the account at account.ranwhat.com. The account itself is free, and so is linking a machine to it with ranwhat login. The cross-machine view, retention and alerts are not written yet, and nor are Team's hash-chained records. Plus subscribers get each of them at the same price as it ships, and Team is not for sale until its records exist. If you want Team now, write to us: you'd be an early customer shaping it, and you should expect to be treated like one.
